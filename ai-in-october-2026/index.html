A five-minute primer · 18 October 2026

Where AI stands in October 2026, and where it's heading

Four charts that explain what today's AI can do, how fast it is improving, and what to expect over the next two years.

TL;DR

  • The best AI systems now outscore PhD experts on hard science exams and can work on their own for hours at a time.
  • The length of task they can finish on their own has doubled roughly every 4–7 months for six years.
  • Free-to-download models trail the best paid ones by only about 3–4 months, at a fraction of the price.
  • The raw computing power once thought to match a human brain is now affordable to a business. The software was the missing piece, and it is catching up fast.

Five words you need

Model
The AI itself: a very large program trained on text, code and images. ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini are apps built on models.
Benchmark
A standard test used to compare models, like an exam with a fixed set of questions or tasks.
Agent
A model that works through a task step by step on its own, using tools such as a web browser or a code editor.
Open model
A model whose files anyone can download and run on their own computer. Closed models are only available through the company's app or paid service.
Compute
Raw computing power, counted as calculations per second. More compute lets you build and run bigger, smarter models.

AI can work alone for longer and longer

The clearest way to measure progress is to ask how long a task an AI agent can finish by itself. A research group called METR times how long each task takes a skilled person, then checks which tasks the AI completes successfully at least half the time.

In 2019 that was a few seconds of work. By early 2026 it was around 12 hours. That growth is remarkably steady on a log scale, where each gridline is a big jump in length.

The newest models have not been measured yet. The test only goes up to about 16 hours of work, and the best models are already near that limit, so everything after early 2026 is a projection.

Who's ahead right now

Seven new top-tier models arrived between mid-September and mid-October. The independent group Artificial Analysis runs every model through the same ten hard tests and combines them into one score. Switch the chart to cost to see the other half of the story: models close to the top are getting much cheaper.

Anthropic's Claude holds the top two spots. OpenAI's GPT-6.1 Sol is the bargain: nearly as capable for about an eighth of the cost. The best open model, Xiaomi's MiMo, scores 46 and costs 13 cents a task.

Free models are only months behind

This chart uses one hard science exam with questions written by PhD researchers. Experts score about 65% in their own field. Both paid and free models passed that line within months of each other in late 2024 and early 2025, and both are now close to the maximum.

Across many tests, researchers at Epoch AI find open models trail the best closed ones by about four months on average. Most of the leading open models now come from Chinese labs.

Computing power keeps getting cheaper

Computing power per dollar doubles about every two and a half years. Scientists' estimates of the computing power a human brain uses vary widely, but the middle estimate is about a thousand trillion calculations per second. Pick a budget to see when it could buy that much.

A business could buy brain-scale raw power years ago. That did not produce human-level AI on its own, because the software was the hard part. The first chart shows that software is now improving quickly too. NVIDIA's next chip, Vera Rubin, is shipping this autumn with about five times the AI computing power of the previous generation, and next year's models will be trained on it.

What to expect next

These are trend projections, not certainties. Each could arrive sooner or later.

Early 2027

A free, downloadable model matches July 2026's best paid model (Claude Opus 5). A version small enough for a high-end desktop follows a month or two later.

Late 2026 – 2027

AI agents finish tasks that take a person a full working week, on their own, about half the time.

Mid 2027 – 2028

Tasks that take a person a month of work become possible. Reliability, not raw ability, becomes the main limit on what people trust AI to do.

2027

The first models trained on the new generation of chips arrive, and the cost of today's top-level AI keeps falling.

Two cautions. Benchmark scores are often reported by the companies themselves, and doing well on a test is not the same as doing a job well. Treat any single number as a signal, not a guarantee.
